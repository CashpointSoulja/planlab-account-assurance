# Decisions

Each decision is mine, with the reason and what I gave up.

| # | Decision | Why | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One segment, five accounts.** | Depth beats breadth for a candidate exercise. Five is enough to show concentration without becoming a list. | Less coverage of other UK infrastructure. |
| 2 | **Two owners and three contractors, overlap shown.** | Real programmes are shared. Showing that NGET, Murphy and M Group share frameworks, and SSEN and Balfour Beatty share Netherton, stops me counting one programme twice. | The "five accounts" headline is deliberately smaller than it sounds. |
| 3 | **The ranking is my judgement, not a score.** | A computed chance-to-close would invent precision I don't have. Each call ("Prioritise", "Test", "Strategic, slower", "Defer behind Balfour") comes with its reasons and stop/route conditions. | Readers must read the reasons rather than sort by a number. |
| 4 | **Contractor-side tests first.** | Owner procurement on critical infrastructure may be slow and security-heavy. My judgement, not verified: a contractor planning team on one named package may be a smaller, faster test of the angle, and may tell me how to approach the owner. | The strongest public trigger (NGET) comes fourth. |
| 5 | **Work the call on NGET anyway.** | It has the clearest public trigger, and it is where a careless opener does the most damage, so it is the best test of tone. | The worked example is not my first-priority account. |
| 6 | **Four labels: Evidence, Hypothesis, Unknown, Blocker.** | Keeps what sources say apart from what I infer and what I don't know. Every pill carries a word, so meaning never relies on colour. | Wordier pages. |
| 7 | **Sponsors are labelled as sponsors.** | A managing director quoted in a press release is not a verified buyer. | No named "decision maker" anywhere. |
| 8 | **Qualification starts Unknown.** | No conversation has happened. Marking criteria as met from public research would be invented qualification. | The pane looks empty by design. |
| 9 | **No AI chat or simulated analysis.** | Planlab's real product has an agent. Imitating it with canned output would misrepresent both the product and my work. | Less "demo" feel. |
| 10 | **Planlab's product layout, not a marketing page.** | It shows I studied the product, and it is the right shape for dense account work: rail, breadcrumb, report, side pane. | Borrowed visual language, so the unofficial label is always visible. |
| 11 | **No licensed fonts shipped.** | HafferXH is commercially licensed to Planlab. I use it only where it is installed locally and bundle Inter (OFL) as the fallback. | The public build is close to, not identical to, Planlab's type. |
| 12 | **Static site, no backend.** | Free hosting, no secrets, nothing to maintain, and practice notes stay in the viewer's browser. | No shared notes or collaboration. |
| 13 | **Spacing-only normalisation of the research, proven by tests.** | The research was supplied as compact notes. I fixed spacing for reading, and a test proves every field still matches once whitespace is removed. | Wording stays as written, even where I'd phrase it differently. |
| 14 | **The current status wins over stale updates.** | On EGL1, the current update says piling is complete, so the older July delay is not a pitch. | Some dramatic angles are dropped. |
