# Jobs to be done and five whys

## Jobs to be done

**The founding SDR's job.** When I prepare to call into a live infrastructure programme, I want to know what public sources say, who might own the schedule and what I don't know. Then I can open with something specific and true, and qualify honestly instead of guessing.

**The hiring team's job.** When we assess a candidate for a founding sales role, we want to see how they research, reason and handle uncertainty on real accounts. Then we can judge them on work rather than claims.

**The prospect's job (as a hypothesis, not a known need).** When delivery assumptions change on a programme I own, I may want to compare options quickly enough to matter for a dated planning or reporting event. Whether that is a real constraint is exactly what discovery has to find out.

## Five whys: why build an evidence-led account plan instead of a pitch?

1. **Why not just write outreach?** Outreach without a verified owner, tool and event is a guess, and a wrong guess spends the prospect's attention.
2. **Why does attention matter so much here?** Owners and contractors in transmission work under security, procurement and regulatory scrutiny. A careless claim about their work can close a door.
3. **Why would a claim be careless?** Public sources show triggers (an Ofgem finding, an award, a phase change), not pain. Turning a trigger into "you have a problem" is an inference the seller hasn't earned.
4. **Why separate the inference from the evidence, then?** So the call can test the inference out loud ("Who owns those scenario re-runs? Is that actually a problem?") and the seller can record unknown rather than inflate qualification.
5. **Why does that matter to a founding team?** Early pipeline data shapes the company's understanding of its market. Honest unknowns and reasons for no-fit are worth more than optimistic meetings.

**Root:** the useful first artefact is a plan that keeps evidence, hypotheses and unknowns apart, and treats five names as the shared programmes they really are.
